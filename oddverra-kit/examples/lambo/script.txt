Lamborghini exists because of a tractor clutch.
After World War Two, an Italian mechanic named Ferruccio Lamborghini built tractors out of leftover army surplus.
Farmers loved them. He got rich! Rich enough to drive Ferraris.
You see, as the story goes, his Ferrari's clutch kept failing.
So his mechanics opened it up and found a clutch much like the ones in his own tractors.
Ferruccio complained to Enzo Ferrari himself, and Enzo, reportedly, told him to stick to tractors.
Big mistake!
In 1963, Ferruccio founded his own car company. The 350 GT arrived in 1964. Then, in 1966, the Miura shocked the world.
Here's the twist. You can still buy a brand-new Lamborghini... tractor.
So one of the most famous supercar names on Earth was born from a bad clutch and a bruised ego.
