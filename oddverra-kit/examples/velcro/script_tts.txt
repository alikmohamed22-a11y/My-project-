Velcro exists because of a dog that kept getting covered in burrs.
In nineteen forty-one, Swiss engineer George de Mestral came home from a hunting trip, and his dog was full of them.
Most people would just pick them off. George put one under a microscope.
You see, every burr is covered in tiny hooks. And hooks grab anything with a loop... like fur. Or fabric.
So George had an idea. A fake burr! Hooks on one strip, loops on the other.
But making it was a nightmare. Cotton hooks wore out fast.
Nylon worked, reportedly once it was sewn under hot infrared light. Then the loops had to be cut just right.
It took him nearly a decade.
He named it after two French words: velours, for velvet, and crochet, for hook. Velcro!
It was patented in the mid-fifties. But the fashion world reportedly turned up its nose. It looked cheap.
Then NASA came calling. On the Apollo missions, it kept tools and snacks from floating away.
So next time you hear that sound... thank a dog who just wouldn't stay out of the weeds.
