The shortest war in history started and ended before ten in the morning.
Zanzibar, August twenty-fifth, eighteen ninety-six. Sultan Hamad bin Thuwaini died suddenly.
Within hours, Khalid bin Barghash moved into the palace and declared himself sultan... without Britain's approval.
Britain, which ran Zanzibar as a protectorate, sent an ultimatum: be out by nine a.m. on the twenty-seventh.
Khalid barricaded himself inside, with guards, cannons, and the royal yacht, the Glasgow.
Nine o'clock came. He stayed.
At two minutes past nine, Royal Navy ships opened fire.
The Glasgow fired back, and was quickly sunk. Her crew was rescued.
By about nine-forty, the palace was wrecked, and the flag came down.
Around five hundred people on the Zanzibari side were killed or wounded. One British sailor was hurt.
The whole war lasted somewhere between thirty-eight and forty-five minutes.
You see, Khalid himself had escaped... to the German consulate. Weeks later, he left by boat, straight from its garden.
And his supporters? They were reportedly made to pay for the shells fired at them.
