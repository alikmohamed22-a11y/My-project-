The man who designed the Pringles can is buried in one. Well... partly.
In the 1950s, Procter and Gamble gave a chemist named Fredric Baur a job: fix the potato chip.
Bags of chips were greasy, half full of air, and packed with broken crumbs.
So Baur's team made every chip the exact same shape: a curved saddle.
Mathematicians call it a hyperbolic paraboloid. It's sturdy, and it stacks perfectly.
Then Baur designed a tall tube to hold the stack.
He filed the patent in 1966, and in 1968, the chips launched in Indiana.
Baur was so proud of that can, he told his family he wanted to be buried in it. They chuckled. He meant it.
You see, when he died in 2008, at 89, his kids kept the promise.
On the way to the funeral home, they stopped at a drugstore for a can... and reportedly argued about the flavor.
They went with Original.
Some of his ashes went into the can, the rest into an urn, buried right beside it.
Talk about being buried in your work.
