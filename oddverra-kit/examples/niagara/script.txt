The first person to go over Niagara Falls in a barrel was a sixty-three-year-old schoolteacher.
You see, Annie Edson Taylor was a widow, and she needed money.
Her plan? Get famous by riding over the Horseshoe Falls. In a barrel.
She had one built of oak and iron, with an anvil in the bottom, and her lucky pillow inside.
But first, a test run. A cat went over. And the cat survived!
So on October twenty-fourth, nineteen-oh-one, her birthday, Annie climbed in.
Friends pumped in air with a bicycle pump, and let her go.
The barrel tipped over the edge... and vanished into the mist.
About twenty minutes later, they hauled it to shore and opened it up. Annie was alive! Just a small cut on her head.
Her verdict, reportedly: "No one ought ever do that again."
Here's the twist. She'd been telling people she was in her forties.
And the fortune? Her manager reportedly ran off with the barrel. Years later, she died poor.
But she got her fame. The first person over Niagara in a barrel... and the first to say: don't.
