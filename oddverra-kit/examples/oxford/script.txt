Oxford University is older than the Aztec Empire.
Sounds wrong? Let's check the dates.
In a little English town called Oxford, teaching existed in some form by 1096.
Then, in 1167, King Henry the Second banned English students from studying in Paris. Many came home, and Oxford grew fast.
Scholars in robes. Candlelit halls. Arguments in Latin.
Meanwhile, across the Atlantic, Tenochtitlan didn't exist yet.
Around 1325, the Mexica founded their city on an island in a lake.
And the Aztec Empire? It began when the Triple Alliance formed, in 1428.
By then, Oxford had been teaching for over 300 years.
So how is that possible?
You see, we file everything old under one word: ancient. But history overlaps in strange ways.
Here's another one. When the Great Pyramid was being built, woolly mammoths were still alive.
The last ones survived on Wrangel Island, in the Arctic, until around 4,000 years ago. Centuries after the pyramid was finished.
So when something sounds ancient, check the dates.
Because Oxford was already old... before the Aztec Empire was even born.
