Bubble Wrap was supposed to go on your walls.
In 1957, in New Jersey, two engineers, Alfred Fielding and Marc Chavannes, sealed two plastic shower curtains together. Their big idea? Textured wallpaper. Modern! Bubbly! ...Nobody bought it.
So they tried again: greenhouse insulation. Bubbles to keep the plants warm. That flopped too.
Two failures. They started a company anyway, Sealed Air, in 1960, and kept hunting for a use.
Then IBM launched a new computer: the 1401. Big, delicate, and it had to be shipped all over the country.
How do you protect a computer? You wrap it in bubbles.
IBM became Bubble Wrap's first big packaging customer, and soon the whole world was wrapping fragile stuff in bubbles.
Today there's even a Bubble Wrap Appreciation Day, the last Monday of January.
So next time you pop one, remember: the world's most satisfying sound came from a wallpaper nobody wanted.
