The Eiffel Tower was supposed to be torn down. In 1909.
Gustave Eiffel built it for the 1889 World's Fair in Paris. Three hundred meters of iron, the tallest structure on Earth.
But his permit only ran for twenty years. After that, the city could pull it down.
And plenty of Parisians wanted that. In 1887, famous artists signed a protest calling it useless and monstrous. A giant black factory chimney.
So how do you save a tower everyone hates?
You see, Eiffel made it useful. He put a weather station at the top, and dropped objects down a cable to study air resistance.
Then came radio. In 1898, a wireless signal flew from the tower to the Pantheon. Eiffel even paid for the army's radio experiments himself.
Suddenly, the ugly tower was the perfect antenna. So in 1910, Paris renewed his permit instead of tearing it down.
And in 1914, its antenna caught German radio messages that helped France at the Battle of the Marne.
Here's the twist. The tower that was meant to last twenty years still carries antennas today. In 2022, a new one made it six meters taller.
The tower Paris wanted gone was saved by something nobody can see. Radio waves.
