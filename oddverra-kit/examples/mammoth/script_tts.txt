Woolly mammoths were still alive when the Great Pyramid was being built.
Not in Egypt, obviously. On a lonely island in the Arctic Ocean: Wrangel Island.
About ten thousand years ago, rising seas cut it off from Siberia, and a few mammoths got stranded.
Scientists think the herd started with no more than eight.
Meanwhile, almost everywhere else, mammoths were dying out. But this little herd held on. It grew to a few hundred, and lasted about six thousand years.
And no, they weren't tiny. That's a myth. They were roughly mainland size.
Then, around four thousand years ago... they vanished. Fast.
So what happened? Hunters? Nope. People reportedly showed up a few centuries too late.
Inbreeding? Their DNA was in rough shape, but a twenty twenty-four study found the herd was slowly weeding out its worst mutations.
You see, a small island has no backup. One sudden disaster, maybe a disease, or rain that froze their food under ice, could take out everyone at once.
So when the last mammoth died, the Great Pyramid was already about five centuries old.
