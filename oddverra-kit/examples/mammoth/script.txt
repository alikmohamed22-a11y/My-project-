Woolly mammoths were still alive when the Great Pyramid was being built.
Not in Egypt, obviously. On a lonely island in the Arctic Ocean: Wrangel Island.
About 10,000 years ago, rising seas cut it off from Siberia, and a few mammoths got stranded.
Scientists think the herd started with no more than eight.
Meanwhile, almost everywhere else, mammoths were dying out. But this little herd held on. It grew to a few hundred, and lasted about 6,000 years.
And no, they weren't tiny. That's a myth. They were roughly mainland size.
Then, around 4,000 years ago... they vanished. Fast.
So what happened? Hunters? Nope. People reportedly showed up a few centuries too late.
Inbreeding? Their DNA was in rough shape, but a 2024 study found the herd was slowly weeding out its worst mutations.
You see, a small island has no backup. One sudden disaster, maybe a disease, or rain that froze their food under ice, could take out everyone at once.
So when the last mammoth died, the Great Pyramid was already about five centuries old.

Facts (checked 2026-11-18):
 - Great Pyramid of Giza, built for Khufu, 4th dynasty; commonly dated to around 2560 BC (~4,500 years ago); Britannica places Khufu's
   dynasty c. 2543-2436 BC and completion in the early 25th century BC, so "about 4,500 years" / "around" wording only.
 - Wrangel Island cut off by rising seas around 10,000 years ago (some sources 10,000-12,000); mainland Siberian mammoths gone by then
   (Science News, 27 Jun 2024; Dehasque et al., Cell 2024 "Temporal dynamics of woolly mammoth genome erosion prior to extinction").
 - Founded by at most ~8 individuals, grew to ~200-300 within ~20 generations and stayed stable ~6,000 years; inbreeding and low
   diversity, mildly harmful mutations accumulated but the most harmful were slowly purged; authors suggest a sudden event (e.g. disease,
   extreme weather) ended them; extinction ~4,000 years ago; humans arrived ~400 years after (Dehasque, via Science News / Live Science).
 - Not dwarfs: Tikhonov, Agenbroad & Vartanyan 2003 and Vartanyan et al. 2008 (Quaternary Research) found Wrangel mammoths comparable
   in size to mainland ones (the 1993 Nature "dwarf" claim was revised).
 - Rain-on-snow icing and drinking-water quality as possible factors: Arppe et al. 2019, Quaternary Science Reviews ("Thriving or
   surviving?", Univ. Helsinki / Tuebingen press releases); last mammoths died out "within a very short time".
 - Pyramid age at extinction: ~2560 BC vs ~2000 BC -> ~500+ years ("about five centuries").
