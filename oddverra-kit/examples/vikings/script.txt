Every horned Viking helmet you've ever seen is wrong.
Archaeologists have found Viking ships, swords, and graves. But not one horned helmet from the Viking Age.
The most complete Viking helmet ever found turned up in Norway in 1943, at a farm called Gjermundbu. It's a rounded iron cap with a guard around the eyes. No horns.
So where did the horns come from?
You see, in 1876, Richard Wagner staged his giant opera cycle, The Ring of the Nibelung, at the very first Bayreuth Festival.
The costume designer, Carl Emil Doepler, put horns on the helmets.
Painters had toyed with the idea before, but the opera made it stick. Soon, horns meant Viking everywhere.
Here's the twist: horned helmets really did exist in Scandinavia. The Veksø helmets, from Denmark, around 900 BC. About 1,700 years before the Vikings.
So the most famous Viking look in history... is basically an opera costume.
