The first speeding ticket ever was for going eight miles an hour. January eighteen ninety-six. Kent, England. Walter Arnold roars through the village of Paddock Wood in his brand-new horseless carriage. His speed? Eight miles an hour. The limit in town? Two. So a police constable hops on his bicycle and gives chase, reportedly for five miles. And he catches him! In court, Arnold faces four charges, including driving without a horse. The fine for speeding? One shilling, plus costs. But that same year, the law changed. The limit jumped to fourteen miles an hour, and drivers celebrated with a run from London to Brighton. You see, they still drive that run every November. All because a cop on a bicycle caught a car doing eight.
