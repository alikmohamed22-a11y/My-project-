Big Ben isn't the tower. It's the bell inside it.
The tower was simply called the Clock Tower. In twenty twelve, it was renamed Elizabeth Tower, for the Queen's Diamond Jubilee.
So why "Big Ben"? Nobody's totally sure. Maybe Sir Benjamin Hall, the big man in charge of the works. Or maybe Ben Caunt, a heavyweight boxer.
But the bell had a rough start.
In eighteen fifty-seven, the first great bell cracked during testing.
So it was broken up, and recast at Whitechapel.
The new bell rang out in eighteen fifty-nine. And within months... it cracked too!
You see, instead of starting over, they turned the bell a quarter-turn, so the hammer hits fresh metal, and gave it a lighter hammer.
And the crack? It's still there. It's reportedly part of why Big Ben sounds the way it does.
Even the clock is fine-tuned with old pennies. Add one to the pendulum, and it gains about two-fifths of a second a day.
So the most famous bell on Earth is a cracked one... and most people still think it's a tower.
