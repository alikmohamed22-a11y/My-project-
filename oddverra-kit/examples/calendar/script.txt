In October 1582, ten days vanished from the calendar. People went to bed on Thursday, October 4th... and woke up on Friday, October 15th.
The culprit? The old Julian calendar. Its year was about eleven minutes too long.
Tiny, right? But over the centuries, those minutes piled up. By the 1500s, Easter had drifted about ten days.
So Pope Gregory the Thirteenth issued a papal bull, Inter gravissimas: skip ten days!
In Italy, Spain, Portugal and Poland, the dates jumped, but the weekdays kept going.
Plus, a new leap-year rule: century years only count if they divide by four hundred.
But not everyone switched. Britain and its colonies, including America, waited until 1752, and lost eleven days. Those famous "give us our eleven days" riots? Probably a myth.
Russia held out until 1918. Greece, until 1923.
You see, Russia was running late. So its famous October Revolution... happened in November, by everyone else's calendar.
