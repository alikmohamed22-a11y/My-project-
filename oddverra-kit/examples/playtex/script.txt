The spacesuit Neil Armstrong wore on the Moon was made by a bra company.
In the nineteen-sixties, NASA needed a suit that could hold air, block heat, and still let an astronaut bend.
The big aerospace firms all wanted the job.
But one bidder was the International Latex Corporation: the company behind Playtex bras and girdles, in Dover, Delaware.
A bra company? Building spacesuits?
And in nineteen sixty-five, it got kicked off the project.
So it asked for one more shot, and reportedly had just six weeks to build a new suit.
In a head-to-head test against two rivals, NASA found its suit was by far the best.
You see, a girdle and a spacesuit do the same tricky job: squeeze tight, and still let you move. Its joints bent like rubber accordions.
So the seamstresses who sewed bras got to work. Up to twenty-one layers per suit. Seams within a sixty-fourth of an inch. One bad stitch could mean a leak.
Then, in July nineteen sixty-nine, Neil Armstrong stepped onto the Moon.
One small step for man. One giant leap for underwear.
