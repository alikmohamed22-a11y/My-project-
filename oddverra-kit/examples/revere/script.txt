Paul Revere never shouted, "The British are coming!" In fact, he never even made it to Concord.
April eighteenth, seventeen seventy-five. British troops are about to march out of Boston to seize the colonists' weapons.
Revere's job: warn the countryside. But quietly. The ride was meant to be secret.
And most colonists still considered themselves British!
So he reportedly warned, "The Regulars are coming out."
Even the two lanterns in the Old North Church weren't for Revere. They signaled patriots across the river, in Charlestown.
And he wasn't alone. William Dawes rode out by another route, and Samuel Prescott joined them on the road.
Then, near Lincoln, a British patrol caught them. Revere was captured. Prescott escaped, and reached Concord.
You see, Revere became famous mostly because of a poem. In eighteen sixty-one, Longfellow's "Paul Revere's Ride" got a lot wrong. No Dawes. No Prescott. No capture.
The rider who actually made it? Almost nobody remembers his name.
