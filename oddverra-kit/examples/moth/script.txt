The first computer bug... was an actual bug.
September ninth, nineteen forty-seven. Harvard University.
The Mark Two is a giant computer: a room full of thousands of clicking switches called relays.
And today, something is wrong.
So the operators go hunting. Panel by panel. Relay by relay.
Until, in Relay number seventy, Panel F, they find it. A moth! Trapped in the switch.
They pull it out, tape it into the logbook, and write: "First actual case of bug being found."
Grace Hopper was on that team, and she loved telling this story. Though reportedly, she wasn't the one who found it.
But why does it say "actual"?
You see, engineers were already calling glitches "bugs." Thomas Edison did it back in the eighteen-seventies.
So the note was a joke. For once, the bug was a real bug.
And that moth is still taped in the logbook, now at the Smithsonian.
The most famous bug in computer history... and it never wrote a single line of code.
