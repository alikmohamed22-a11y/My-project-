Your microwave exists because a candy bar melted in a man's pocket.
It's nineteen forty-five. Percy Spencer, a Raytheon engineer who'd barely gone to school, is working beside a magnetron, the tube that powers radar.
Then he notices something. The candy bar in his pocket? Melted.
Most people would grab a napkin. Percy grabbed popcorn.
He held the kernels near the tube... and they popped all over the lab!
Next, he reportedly tried an egg. It exploded, right on a coworker.
You see, those invisible microwaves make the water inside food jiggle, and all that jiggling is heat.
Raytheon filed a patent that same year. And by nineteen forty-seven, they were selling the first microwave oven: the Radarange.
It stood nearly six feet tall, weighed about seven hundred fifty pounds, and reportedly cost around five thousand dollars.
It took twenty years to shrink it onto a kitchen counter: the Amana Radarange, in nineteen sixty-seven.
And Percy's reward? Reportedly, the same two-dollar bonus Raytheon paid for every patent.
Two dollars... for the machine that pops your popcorn.
