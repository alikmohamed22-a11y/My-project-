Fortune cookies aren't Chinese. They most likely came from Japan.
Researcher Yasuko Nakamachi found an eighteen seventy-eight Japanese book showing a baker making tsujiura senbei: fortune crackers, with the fortune tucked in the fold.
And family bakeries near Kyoto still bake them by hand today.
So how did they end up in America?
You see, Japanese immigrants likely brought them to California.
In San Francisco, Makoto Hagiwara reportedly served them at the Japanese Tea Garden. But in Los Angeles, Seiichi Kito claimed them too.
In nineteen eighty-three, a mock court in San Francisco "ruled" for San Francisco. Los Angeles was not impressed.
Then came World War Two. Japanese Americans were forced into internment camps, and their bakeries closed.
Chinese American bakeries took over making the cookies, and that's reportedly why we link them to China.
Here's the twist. In China itself, fortune cookies are barely known. A company that tried selling them there in nineteen ninety-two reportedly gave up. Too American.
So the cookie that predicts your future... got its own past wrong.
