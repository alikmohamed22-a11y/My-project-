Napoleon wasn't short. You were tricked... by a cartoonist.
When he died in 1821, his doctor measured him: five foot two. Tiny, right?
Except those were French feet and inches, and the French inch was longer than the British one.
Convert it, and Napoleon was about five foot seven. Average... maybe even a little tall for a Frenchman back then.
So where did "short" come from? London.
A British cartoonist named James Gillray, who never even saw Napoleon in person, drew him as a tiny, tantrum-throwing little man. "Little Boney." And Britain loved it.
It didn't help that Napoleon was often surrounded by his Imperial Guard: giant soldiers in towering fur hats.
And his troops called him "the little corporal"... but that was affection, not a measurement.
Napoleon conquered half of Europe. But he never beat the cartoon.
Two hundred years later, we still call bossy short guys... a "Napoleon complex."
