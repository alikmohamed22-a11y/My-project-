# Swap generic tags for keywords and series tags

Hashtags in late 2026 mostly label a video. They do little for reach. Instagram now caps you at **5 hashtags per Reel**, and its head, Adam Mosseri, says hashtags don't increase reach. YouTube's own Shorts team says tags have "lost reach value." TikTok says more hashtags "doesn't always mean better." What actually helps a new account like Sam Fakes It is putting search phrases people really type ("can't cook," "fake chef," "lied on my résumé," "animated comedy") in three places: the first caption line, the on-screen title card and Sam's first spoken line. Hashtags then play a small supporting role. Use one unique series tag, **#SamFakesIt**, on every platform, plus 2–4 mid-size niche tags such as #2danimation, #animatedseries, #restaurantlife and #comedyskit. Skip huge tags like #comedy, #funny and #chef, where a new post sinks within minutes. Using a single #comedy on YouTube is within the rules, but it wastes the visible slot. Replace it with #SamFakesIt plus one or two topic tags, and put the keywords in the title. Copy-paste sets for Day 1 are below.

## Every platform now punishes stuffing and rewards keywords

**Instagram** cut the cap from 30 to **5 hashtags per post or Reel**, announced by @creators on Dec 18, 2025. Instagram said "fewer (up to 5) more targeted hashtags, rather than many generic ones" work better, and singled out #reels and #explore as weak ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-implements-new-limits-on-hashtag-use/808309/); [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/no-more-hashtag-stuffing-instagram-limits-hashtag-usage-to-just-5-per-reel-and-post-507444-2025-12-19)). Mosseri said in 2025 that hashtags "don't work" for reach but help tell people what a post is about. You also can't follow hashtags anymore; that ended in Dec 2024 ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagrams-removing-option-follow-hashtags/)). Instagram search matches the username, profile name, bio, captions and hashtags. Mosseri's 2021 guidance is to put keywords **"in the caption, not the comments"** ([Instagram](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/break-down-how-instagram-search-works/)). Since July 2025, public posts from professional accounts can also show up in Google Search, so your caption doubles as web SEO ([Planoly](https://planoly.com/blog/instagram-posts-are-now-showing-up-in-google-search)). Some guides say hashtags in comments count toward the 5; Meta hasn't confirmed this.

**TikTok** publishes **no official hashtag cap**. Its Creator Academy says hashtags "can support discovery, but more doesn't always mean better," and that relevant, accurate language matters most ([TikTok Creator Academy](https://www.tiktok.com/creator-academy/article/Getting-discovered-with-Search)). Planoly reported a 5-hashtag cap in late 2025 without citing TikTok, and other guides call that claim copied from Instagram ([Planoly](https://planoly.com/blog/tiktok-5-hashtag-limit); [HowSociable](https://howsociable.com/tiktok-hashtags)). Staying at 5 or fewer avoids the question entirely. The data points the same way. A June 2026 study of about 60K matured posts found TikTok videos with **0 hashtags had 304 median views, versus 140 for videos with 7–10** ([quso.ai](https://quso.ai/research/do-hashtags-help)). This is correlation, not proof, but it argues against stuffing. The one controlled keyword test put search terms in the script, the caption and on-screen text, and got **+33% For You views** on TikTok ([Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/experiment-social-seo/)).

**YouTube Shorts** places hashtags in the title or description. Up to **3 description hashtags show above the title**, and YouTube picks which ones. If a video has **more than 60, YouTube ignores all of them**, and misleading or excessive tags can get a video pulled from search ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6390658)). In July 2026, YouTube's own Shorts tips said tags have lost reach value ([Social Media Today](https://socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-offers-tips-on-working-with-shorts-videos/825096)). No official source says #Shorts is required. A Short is classified by its vertical format and length, so the tag is optional.

| Platform | Hard limit | Use | Biggest lever |
|---|---|---|---|
| Instagram Reels | 5 | 4–5 | Caption line 1, profile name, on-screen text |
| TikTok | None official | 4–5 | Spoken + on-screen + first ~100 caption characters |
| YouTube Shorts | 60 (all ignored above) | 2–3 | Keyword title |

## Mid-size niche tags beat the giant ones

Tag sizes below are Instagram counts from trackers, pulled Nov 9, 2026. Different trackers sometimes disagree by up to 50x, so treat them as rough sizes only. I could not get reliable TikTok counts. The **giant tags are useless for a 0-follower account**: #comedy has about **62M posts and 252K new uses a week**, and #funny gets about **3,458 posts an hour** ([best-hashtags #comedy](https://best-hashtags.com/hashtag/comedy/); [#funny](https://best-hashtags.com/hashtag/funny/)). #chef (33.6M) and #cooking (49M) are giant too, and full of real food that your audience doesn't want ([#chef](https://best-hashtags.com/hashtag/chef/)).

The useful band is tags that are active but not flooded:

| #fyp, #viral, #explore, #reels | 54M–144M | ❌ Skip on IG; one #fyp on TikTok is optional at most |

**Avoid** tags that third-party lists flag as restricted on Instagram, some of which sound like a fit: **#adulting, #hustler, #killingit, #workflow, #hardworkpaysoff**, plus engagement bait like #followforfollow ([Ayrshare](https://www.ayrshare.com/blog/avoid-using-these-instagram-banned-hashtags/)). Also skip fandom tags such as #bobsburgers, which count as misuse on unrelated content. On TikTok, one 2026 study saw videos tagged #fyp do about 17% better than their account's median, but its authors call it correlational ([SocialCrawl](https://www.socialcrawl.dev/blog/do-hashtags-work-on-tiktok)). Use #fyp only if you have a slot to spare.

**#SamFakesIt** is uncontested, so its tag page becomes your episode archive. This copies what the 2D series Weird Helga does with #weirdhelga ([CraveFX](https://cravefx.com/blog/animated-videos-on-tiktok-is-it-worth-posting/)). No source shows a branded tag raises reach, though. Use it as a label, and drive bingeing with Instagram's **Link a Reel** ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/instagram-brings-linked-reels-heres-what-it-means-and-how-you-can-use-it-490683-2025-08-22)), TikTok Playlists, and YouTube's related-video link plus a numbered playlist ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-related-links-connect-shorts-long-videos)). Per-episode tags like #day1 waste a slot.

## Day 1 copy-paste sets

The keyword phrases below are my picks. Before posting, check them against TikTok and Instagram search autocomplete. Put **"DAY 1/30"** plus a keyword title card on screen in the first second, and have Sam say the premise out loud ("I lied on my résumé that I'm a chef…"). Burned-in subtitles double as on-screen keywords.

**Instagram Reels (5 tags)**
```
Day 1 of 30: I lied on my résumé that I'm a chef… and the fancy restaurant hired me. I can't cook. 🍳😬
Animated comedy series. Follow to see if Sam survives 30 days of faking it.
#SamFakesIt #2danimation #animatedseries #restaurantlife #comedyskit
```
Possible swaps: #chefslife for #restaurantlife, or #animationmeme for #comedyskit. Set the profile name to **"Sam Fakes It | Animated Comedy"** so the name field is searchable too.

**TikTok (5 tags)**
```
Day 1 of 30 faking being a chef 🍳 I lied on my résumé, got hired at a fancy restaurant, and I can't cook. Animated comedy series — Day 2 tomorrow.
#SamFakesIt #animation #storytime #chefsoftiktok #animationtiktok
```
#chefsoftiktok and #animationtiktok are tags TikTok creators actually use, but I found no counts for them. Pin Day 1 to the profile, and start a "Sam Fakes It: Day 1–30" playlist once your account has the feature.

**YouTube Shorts (3 tags)**
```
Title: Day 1 of 30 Faking Being a Chef (I Can't Cook) | Sam Fakes It
Description: I lied on my résumé that I'm a chef and got hired at a fancy restaurant. Animated comedy series, 30 days.
#SamFakesIt #animation #storytime
```
Keep hashtags out of the title; reportedly that hides the row above the title ([Sprout Social](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/YouTube-hashtags/)). Adding #Shorts is optional. Add Day 1 to a numbered "Sam Fakes It" playlist, and once Day 2 is up, link Day 1 to it with the related-video link.

**About your old single #comedy on YouTube:** one tag is fully allowed, and one to three is the sweet spot. #comedy is the wrong one, though: it's huge and generic, and it fills the visible slot above your title with a tag nobody browses. Swap it for **#SamFakesIt + #animation (+ #storytime)**, and let the keyword-rich title do the search work.

## Conclusion

Hashtag choice now matters far less than wording. What drives discovery is the same phrase ("fake chef," "can't cook," "lied on résumé") repeated across the speech, the screen, the caption's first line and the title. The real competitive edge is consistency: identical "Day N of 30" labels, one series tag, and native episode links, so a viewer who lands on Day 7 can binge back to Day 1. Two things are still unknown: TikTok tag sizes, and whether Instagram counts comment hashtags toward the 5. Check both in-app, and after about 10 episodes, use your own analytics to compare tags rather than any vendor's study.
